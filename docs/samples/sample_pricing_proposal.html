PRELIMINARY — DO NOT SEND CUSTOMER: HOLD - MODEL

In short

You are buying 3 managed reinforcement-learning (RL) cycles to improve Held-out pass rate for Internal workflow automation, from a measured baseline of 60% toward a target of 80%, over 6 weeks. The model is Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, an open-weight model with 35 billion parameters, of which 3 billion are active for each token. The total is $210,000. The 80% target is a review point, not a guarantee: when it is reached, or when results stop improving, you decide whether to continue. You approve every cycle, and each invoice is issued when its milestone completes.

Where your money goes

  • Eval and environment foundation $30,000 (14%)
  • Engineering through the cycles $78,000 (37%)
  • 3 RL cycles $72,000 (34%)
  • Handoff and independent verification $30,000 (14%)

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–2

Eval and environment foundation

$30,000

Why it costs this

This is the foundation every RL cycle depends on: an evaluation you can trust, a grader that measures what matters, and a baseline to measure improvement against. You own these assets and can reuse them with any future model. Self-serve RL services bill for compute only, which leaves your team to build them; here we build them with you and price the engineering time: 108 hours across four roles, delivered in the first two weeks. The expected improvement range is set from the measured baseline at the end of this phase; we do not promise a figure in advance.

Words used in this proposal

RL cycle
One round of training the model on its own attempts, scoring the attempts, and updating the model.
Rollout
One attempt by the model at a task, recorded with its result.
Held-out evaluation
A test set the model never trains on, used to measure real improvement.
Grader
The rules that score an attempt, for example pass or fail, or a rubric score.
Compute allowance
The training compute included in the price, stated in GPU-hours for the whole engagement.
Checkpoint
A saved version of the trained model, with its exact settings, so results can be reproduced.
Reinforcement learning (RL)
A way of improving a model by letting it attempt tasks, scoring the attempts, and rewarding the behaviour that scores well.
Reward
The score the grader gives an attempt, which the training uses to decide what to reinforce.
Baseline
The score of the untrained model on the held-out evaluation. Improvement is measured from this point.
Metric
The single measure of success you approve, for example the held-out pass rate.
Go / no-go gate
A decision point at the end of the foundation phase. You approve it before any RL cycle is paid for.
Standard-size model
The reference tier that the base cycle price is set for. Smaller and larger models are priced as a multiple of it.
Weights
The learned numbers that make up the model. The handoff package includes the trained weights.
Traces
Recorded attempts, showing each step the model took, for inspection.
Runbook
Written instructions for operating the handed-over model.
Forward-deployed engineer
An engineer who works alongside your team on-site or in your environment, rather than only remotely.
Change order
A short written amendment that adds or changes scope and its price before the work starts.
Statement of work (SOW)
The contract schedule that defines scope, phases, evidence, invoices and amendment rules. Sent with this proposal.

Common questions

Why do I pay for the foundation before training starts?

Training is only as good as the test that measures it. The foundation builds that test, the grader and a baseline, and you keep them. It is the one part of the work that makes every later cycle measurable.

Why is engineering separate from the cycles?

Engineers keep the loop running whether or not a given cycle improves the score. Their effort follows the number of cycles, so it is billed with them.

Why does the price depend on the model?

Larger models need more compute and more rollouts to learn the same task, so each cycle costs more to run. The price tier reflects that.

What if a cycle does not improve the score?

You decide after each cycle whether to continue, change direction or stop. You pay for the cycles and milestones completed. We do not stop contracted work silently, and we do not charge for work you have stopped before it starts.

What happens if I stop early?

You pay for approved work completed and for any non-cancellable third-party compute committed for your engagement, net of reasonable reuse. The handoff fee is billed only on a signed handoff.
