Statement of work

The contract terms the proposal depends on: what is in scope now, how the customer keeps control as the engagement grows, and when the deal is re-papered.

Review page. The statement of work below is the customer copy, marked as a draft until legal review.

Intent

Commit only to a defined scope now, with objective acceptance and milestone invoices. As the work grows, explicit triggers make expansion a new order, not a silent change in scope.

Short term and long term

Now (this order)As the customer expands
Scope is one workflow and one model.A new model, method, region or topology needs a new order.
The customer decides after each cycle whether to continue.Production is a separate order with its own envelope.
Acceptance rests on objective evidence (run IDs, graded metrics, a signed handoff).Scale reviews start at 80% of the envelope, before capacity runs out.
Invoices follow signed milestones; early exit pays approved work and committed third-party compute.Any change of scope goes through the amendment triggers in section 5.

Thought process

  1. Stop rules belong to the customer. A score can prompt a review, but it never stops contracted work on its own. This keeps the customer in control without giving them a free exit after the work is paid for.
  2. Pay for finished work. Milestones match the phases in the proposal, so the invoice and the deliverable line up one for one.
  3. Re-paper on named triggers. Section 5 of the SOW lists the triggers. Each one is a reason to re-scope and re-price the work.
