Pricing model: from cost to price

The price starts from what it costs to deliver. The people fees are fixed; the RL cycles are priced from the compute they consume and the GPU they run on. This page shows the chain, and how the GPU choice, model size and cycle count move the price.

2. GPU type

A GPU type moves cost through two things: its hourly rate and how much work an hour buys. The table compares cost per unit of work, not the sticker rate, because a faster GPU can cost less per unit of work even at a higher hourly price. Existing capacity only counts for the matching GPU type, so switching type can also change which weeks are covered and how they are bought.

5. Thought process

  1. Cost sets the floor. The RL cycle line is the only part of the price that follows compute cost. The people fees are fixed, so the price reads as a stable rate card to the customer.
  2. GPU type matters through cost per unit of work. Compare the last column of the GPU table, not the hourly rate.
  3. Scope matters more than hardware at this size. On the example deal GPU cost is under a tenth of COGS. In the compute-heavy stress case it is a quarter to a third, and the GPU choice then moves the price a lot.
  4. Negotiate on scope, not on GPU. If the customer pushes on price, the gap to the cost-driven price is the room. Trading cycles or workflow scope keeps the economics intact. Cutting the price against an unconfirmed GPU rate does not: if the rate is wrong, the margin goes with it.
