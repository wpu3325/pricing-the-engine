Intent

Win the engagement on scope and evidence, not on a lower rate. Every fee pays for something the customer can inspect (an evaluation, a grader, a cycle report, a signed handoff), so the question “why this much?” has a concrete answer for each line.

Thought process

  1. Price from cost, not from competitors. The internal cost model sets a floor; the price is set so the margin target holds. The rationale page gives the numbers and the reasons for the target and floor.
  2. Fund the foundation first. The readiness phase is the largest single fee before any training. It builds the evaluation and grader that every later cycle depends on, and it can be reused with another model.
  3. Make each cycle a visible decision. Cycle fees are explicit, so the customer decides to continue or stop after each cycle knowing what the next one costs.
  4. Defend the price on scope. If a competitor is cheaper, match scope rather than rate. The pricing and margin page shows the margin left after a match and the walk-away price.
